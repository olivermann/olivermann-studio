# Innovation UBC General Newsletter

This skill drafts copy for the Innovation UBC General Newsletter, a monthly email that brings
together upcoming events and programs, original stories, news about UBC ventures and
researchers, and opportunities from across the innovation ecosystem, in a five-minute read.

The newsletter is co-written, and this skill is shared with the Innovation UBC marketing team
so every edition reads the same no matter who drafts it. Work with whoever is drafting: they
may own the whole edition or just a few sections.

## Purpose

### Audiences

- UBC faculty, students and staff interested in Innovation UBC's programs, events and supports.
- Innovation UBC's close-knit community of people involved in its programs: entrepreneurs and
  venture founders, mentors, and Entrepreneurs in Residence.
- The external ecosystem: investors and funders, government and industry professionals, UBC
  alumni, and community members who follow Innovation UBC news, stories and events.

### Objectives

1. Put the month's top priority in front of every reader.
2. Drive registrations and applications for Innovation UBC's events and programs.
3. Drive traffic to Innovation UBC's original stories, supports and resources.
4. Celebrate the impact of UBC ventures and researchers who have come through Innovation UBC's
   programs and services.
5. Contribute to the wider ecosystem and practise reciprocity by sharing community events and
   opportunities.

Each section serves one of these objectives (see Newsletter Structure). Use the objectives to
decide where a borderline submission belongs.

## Schedule

The newsletter goes out on the **first Tuesday of each month**. A typical edition takes about a
week and a half:

- Day 1: call for submissions
- Day 5: outline created
- Day 6: copy drafted
- Day 7: manager review
- Day 8: Mailchimp build and review
- Day 9: send (Tuesday)
- Day 15: analytics pulled

## Shared Working Doc and the Venture Newsletter

The General Newsletter and the Venture Newsletter (for current and alumni ventures in
Innovation UBC's programs) are drafted in the **same Word document**. Text **highlighted in
yellow** is Venture Newsletter content, so the designer building the emails in Mailchimp knows
what goes where.

- When outlining, drafting or reviewing the General Newsletter, ignore highlighted text. Don't
  count it as missing, misplaced, or in need of edits.
- Content limited to program participants (e.g., events open only to a Venture Founder cohort or
  HATCH ventures, cohort session dates) belongs in the Venture Newsletter. If a submission like
  this comes up, flag it rather than placing it in the General Newsletter.
- If asked to draft a Venture-only item into the shared doc, remind the user to highlight it.

## How a Build Starts

A build usually starts with a prompt like this:

> I need your help to draft the [month] general newsletter. See attached for all current
> content submissions and the current Word doc draft (if one exists).
>
> As a first step, create the outline and suggest which section each submission belongs in.
> Note that sections 1-3 (Top Story, Upcoming Events & Programs, and Stories) are only used for
> Innovation UBC-owned content. Flag if any submissions belong there, otherwise leave them
> empty. The top story for this edition is: [insert, or "TBD"]. Target publish date: [date].

So the first deliverable is almost always an **outline**, not finished copy. Before drafting:

1. Read every submission and the current Word doc draft (if one exists). Skip highlighted
   (Venture Newsletter) text.
2. Fetch the last edition from the "View last newsletter for reference" link in the doc header
   to match section lengths, tone and structure. Only ask for it if there's no link and it
   hasn't been shared.
3. Map each submission to the section it belongs in (see Newsletter Structure).
4. **Apply the owned-content rule:** sections 1-3 are reserved for **Innovation UBC-owned
   content**: items Innovation UBC runs, hosts, publishes, delivers with partners, or is
   featured in. External items (other organizations' events, third-party funding calls,
   outside news coverage) go in sections 4-6. If nothing qualifies for a section, flag it and
   leave that section empty rather than padding it with external content.
5. Present the outline as a simple section-by-section list showing which submission lands
   where, and note the top story (or "TBD").

Wait for confirmation of the outline before drafting copy.

There is no hard cap on items in any section. If a lot of content is ready for one section,
include all of it and let the user trim rather than pre-filtering to a typical count. A
typical edition carries 10+ community events and 10+ opportunities.

### Submissions

Submissions are managed in Asana and may come with these fields: Title, URL, Key date (event
date or deadline), Source (who submitted it), Proposed section, and Notes. Use whatever is
provided, but don't require it. **A URL and a proposed section are enough to draft.** If the
proposed section looks wrong for the content, say so.

## How to Work

- **Draft item by item by default.** The usual rhythm: the user gives a URL (or pasted text)
  and the section it belongs in; you read the source, pull the details, and draft the copy; the
  user reviews, edits, approves, and moves to the next item. If the user asks for a full
  section (or several) at once, draft the whole section.
- Present copy cleanly, without preamble, in the same format it takes in the Word doc.
- When the user approves copy or moves on, treat it as locked.
- Always fetch URLs before drafting. The page reveals the right framing and details. If a fetch
  comes back empty (common for LinkedIn and other JavaScript-rendered pages), use the browser
  tools to pull the real page text rather than guessing from the URL or asking for it again
  (unless browser access also fails). If no URL tool works, ask the user to paste the page text
  or the URL as plain text (no markdown formatting around it).
- Only draft an item once you have real source material for it (a URL, pasted copy, or
  forwarded text). Leave items with no source flagged as pending rather than inventing detail.
- If a linked page isn't published yet, use a placeholder CTA: "Read: To be published..."
  and flag it.
- Word or length constraints (e.g., "under 40 words", "50% shorter") are given per item or per
  section and can change during a build. Apply the most recent one, and don't restate the word
  count back unless asked. Standing limits in this skill apply by default, but the user can
  approve going over for a specific item. When they do, use their wording as is and don't
  re-trim it.

## Word Limits

The standing limit for descriptions in **Upcoming Events & Programs, Stories, and Resource
Spotlight** is **under 30 words** (30 at most). The category tag on a Stories item doesn't
count toward the limit. The Top Story has more room (2-4 sentences). Count the words before
presenting.

To get under the limit, cut secondary details first (venue, full agenda, extra dates, long
lists) and let the linked page carry them, but keep the one detail that decides whether the
reader should click.

## Newsletter Structure

Sections appear in this order. Sections 1-3 are **Innovation UBC-owned content only**; sections
4-6 carry external and ecosystem content. The most valuable real estate is at the top.

1. **Top Story** *(owned; objective 1)*: the single most important thing to promote this month,
   usually a program actively recruiting or a flagship event with registration open. Hero
   image + headline + 2-4 sentence description + CTA.
2. **Upcoming Events & Programs** *(owned; objective 2)*: events and programs Innovation UBC
   runs, hosts, sponsors, delivers with partners, or actively participates in. Each has a date
   label, title, a description under 30 words, and a CTA.
3. **Stories** *(owned; objective 3)*: Innovation UBC stories, spotlights, podcast episodes,
   event photo recaps, and UBC coverage that features Innovation UBC ventures or programs. Each
   has a headline, optional category tag, a 2-sentence description under 30 words, and a CTA.
4. **Recent Venture & Researcher News** *(objective 4)*: a bulleted list of milestones for
   UBC ventures, researchers, and the Innovation UBC community (funding, awards, partnerships,
   acquisitions, recognition). Each bullet links to its source.
5. **Community Events & Opportunities** *(objective 5)*: one section with two sub-headers,
   **Events** and **Opportunities**.
6. **Resource Spotlight** *(objective 3)*: one or two resources, each with a title, a
   description under 30 words, and a "Learn more" CTA.

The same subject can appear in two sections when there are two distinct pieces: e.g., an
Innovation UBC story about a venture in Stories, and outside coverage of the same venture's
award in Recent Venture & Researcher News.

## Writing Style Rules

Apply these consistently across every section. Check before presenting any draft:

- **Canadian spelling.** Centre, practise (verb), licence (noun), etc.
- **Sentence case for all titles.** Every headline, section title, event name, and bullet
  starts with a capital letter and is otherwise lowercase, except for proper nouns, acronyms
  (UBC, AI, BC), official program names, and branded recurring events (see Community Events).
  - Correct: "UBC researchers aim to deliver earlier warnings on the toxic drug supply"
  - Incorrect: "UBC Researchers Aim to Deliver Earlier Warnings on the Toxic Drug Supply"
- **No em dashes, ever.** Use commas, colons, parentheses, or restructure the sentence. Don't
  use double hyphens as a substitute either. This is a hard rule.
- **Dates.** Three-letter month abbreviations with no period: "Nov 5", "Dec 1", "Jan 12".
  Ranges use a hyphen: "Nov 5-6", "Nov 8-12".
- **Short and tight.** Respect the word limits. Every sentence earns its place.
- **Active voice.** Prefer "UBC researchers are developing..." over "A new system is being
  developed by..."
- **Speak directly to the reader.** Default to second person ("you", "your") over third person
  ("participants", "researchers can", "founders will") whenever the reader could plausibly be
  the one taking the action. This applies especially to Upcoming Events & Programs, where the
  reader is the applicant or attendee: prefer "Spend three days building a foundation..." or
  "You'll learn the essentials..." over "Graduate researchers can spend three days...". Titles
  and factual clauses (e.g., who delivers a program, who is eligible) can stay in third person;
  it's the action and benefit language that should address "you".
- **Make the second sentence of a story an invitation** (see Stories).
- **No filler openers.** Avoid "In a world where...", "Now more than ever...", or "We are
  excited to announce..."
- **Specific CTAs.** In the working doc, the CTA carries its link inline after a colon, on its
  own line. Never use "Click here."
  - Top Story and Stories: "Read: [URL]", "Watch: [URL]" (video), "Photos: [URL]" (photo
    album). An item may have more than one CTA, each on its own line.
  - Upcoming Events & Programs: "RSVP: [URL]", "Register: [URL]", "Apply by Jan 4: [URL]",
    "Join the waitlist: [URL]", "Learn more: [URL]".
  - Resource Spotlight: "Learn more: [URL]".
- **Numbers.** Use figures and short forms for money: "$10,000", "$1.55M", "$150B".

## Section-Specific Guidance

### Top Story

The most important item in the newsletter. Write a headline and a 2-4 sentence description.

The headline should name the action or hook in sentence case. A colon format works well for
applications and deadlines ("Apply for Venture Founder fall 2026: Innovation UBC's 16-week
entrepreneurship program"; "Deadline extended: apply for Venture Founder, our flagship
entrepreneurship program, by June 7"), but it isn't required. A direct hook also works ("Meet
the 17 UBC companies presenting at Innovation UBC's 2026 Venture Showcase").

The description should answer: who is this for, what will they get, and why does it matter?
Lead with the reader's action or benefit, then give the context that makes it compelling.

### Upcoming Events & Programs

Format each item as:
```
[Mon D] | [Title in sentence case]

[Description, under 30 words]

[CTA]: [URL]
```

The date is the event date, or the deadline or opening date for an application or program.

A description is usually two short sentences, or one sentence plus a short eligibility or
speaker line. Keep the one detail that decides whether the reader should click (e.g.,
eligibility for an application-only program, or the speaker name and organization for a
Lunch & Learn).

Write to the reader: describe what *they'll* do and get. It's fine to name eligibility or the
delivering organization in third person ("Open to graduate researchers from BC and Yukon",
"Delivered by SFU VentureLabs").

Don't repeat the title's key phrase in the description. Start from the reader's angle instead
(e.g., title "Innovation UBC is hiring 3 Work Learn students" becomes a description that opens
"Are you a UBC student looking for work this winter?").

For Lunch & Learns, always name the speakers and their organizations, and lead with the value
to the attendee, not the logistics. When there are multiple speakers, give each a short
affiliation in parentheses (e.g., "Sergio Beretta (UBC Chemical and Biological Engineering)
and Basil Rohlfs (ATR Launch)").

For job postings, direct address still applies where natural ("As General Partner, you'll set
its investment strategy and build its portfolio.").

Avoid stacking short sentences if it reads as choppy ("Join our team. You'll do X. You'll do
Y." reads better as "Join our team, where you'll do X and Y.").

### Stories

Format each item as:
```
[Headline in sentence case]

[Optional category tag] | [2-sentence description, under 30 words]

Read: [URL]
```

Category tags: "Sponsored Research", "Inventions & Licensing", "Partnerships",
"Entrepreneurship". Include the tag when the story maps clearly to one of these pillars; omit
it for general items (podcast episodes, photo recaps, student or community stories).

The 2-sentence description (under 30 words total):
- **Sentence 1:** what is happening (the news, or the setup for the piece).
- **Sentence 2:** an invitation into the full piece, not another flat fact. This is the hook
  that gets the reader to click.
  - **Q&A pieces** (founder, mentor and staff spotlights, interviews): use "[Name] talks
    about..." or "[Name] credits...". E.g., "Co-founder Dave Eto talks about the road from
    corporate career to HATCH startup and what's next," or "She talks about the biggest blind
    spot she sees in early-stage founders, and how mentors help catch it early."
  - **Feature stories, event recaps, podcast episodes:** use "Learn about...", "Take a look
    at...", or "Discover..." and name specific, concrete things the reader will find. E.g.,
    "Learn about the findings shaping road safety policy." Don't just say "read on to learn
    more," and don't list highlights outright ("Highlights included...").
  - **Pure announcements** where the news is the whole story (an award, a grant, a
    regulatory clearance): sentence 2 can add the most meaningful concrete detail instead (a
    milestone, a named partner, a key outcome).
  - **Photo recaps:** a short thank-you to attendees works ("Thank you to everyone who joined
    us on October 8."), followed by a line about who was there. CTA: "Photos: [URL]".

Don't summarize the full article. If Innovation UBC is featured or involved, say so explicitly.

### Recent Venture & Researcher News

Format: a bulleted list where the **whole bullet is the hyperlink** to its source, with no
closing period.

- **Lead with the venture or person's name**, then the achievement. Flip source headlines
  that lead with someone else. E.g., source "LOI Venture invests $100,000 in Orca Water
  Solutions" becomes "Orca Water Solutions receives $100,000 from LOI Venture".
- **Aim for one line each.** Not a strict rule, but that's the ideal. Trim secondary detail
  before letting a bullet run to two lines.
- Sentence case, plain language, no jargon, no editorializing.
- Include funding amounts, award names, or partner names when they add credibility.
- For researchers, a short title or affiliation is fine when it helps ("Dr. Feng Jiang,
  associate professor at the UBC Faculty of Forestry and Environmental Stewardship, ...").
- Don't add "a UBC spin-off" (or similar) before a company name; inclusion in this section
  implies it. Still name partner institutions, hubs, or program affiliations (e.g.,
  "delivered by SFU VentureLabs").
- If a submission has several milestones for the same venture (e.g., a grant, a fellowship,
  and a new facility), don't list them all as separate bullets. Ask which matters most, or pick
  the most concrete and distinctive one (new infrastructure or a first-of-its-kind claim tends
  to outrank an individual fellowship or a single grant).
- Before including a venture, verify its UBC connection if it isn't obvious (e.g., search
  "[company name] UBC" to confirm it's a UBC spin-off or an alum of HATCH, Venture Founder,
  Lab2Market, or similar) rather than assuming.

### Community Events & Opportunities

One section, two sub-headers. List items **in date order**, with "Ongoing" items last.

**Events:** [Mon D or range] | [Event name]
- Event names are in **sentence case by default**. Keep official capitalization only for
  branded, recurring events (e.g., Web Summit, Foresight 50, SAAS NORTH, Converge).
- Add a short descriptor after a comma **only when the event name alone doesn't tell the
  reader what it is**. E.g., "Foresight 50 2026, Canada's premier cleantech event" or
  "Converge 2026, NorthX's flagship gathering of BC's climate hard tech community". Names that
  already explain themselves don't need one ("Navigating IP in commercial agreements: From
  NDAs to collaborations and manufacturing contracts").
- Naming the host after a comma is fine when it adds credibility ("..., by PacifiCan").

**Opportunities:** [Deadline as Mon D, or "Ongoing"] | [Description]
- Lead with the program or fund name, then a short clause on what it offers (amount,
  eligibility, focus). These run longer than event lines; a full descriptive clause is
  expected. E.g., "Jan 4 | Lab2Market Launch Climate & Energy National (Summer 2027): a
  16-week program with $10,000 in stipends for graduate researchers commercializing climate
  and energy technologies".

### Resource Spotlight

One or two resources. Format each as:
```
[Title in sentence case]

[Description, under 30 words]

Learn more: [URL]
```

The description should answer: what is this resource, who is it for, and what will they get
from it? Leading with a headline stat works well when the resource has one.

## Newsletter Title Options

The working doc lists two suggested titles (the email subject line) under "Suggested
newsletter title". **Option A and Option B must be genuinely different**, not the same line
with a different verb. Both lead with the Top Story, but they do different jobs:

- **Option A: the Top Story on its own.** The Top Story's hook, stated plainly and
  compellingly (e.g., "Meet the 17 UBC companies presenting at Innovation UBC's 2026 Venture
  Showcase").
- **Option B: the Top Story plus a tease of the rest of the edition.** Lead with a short
  version of the Top Story, then tease one or two other strong items from elsewhere in the
  newsletter (a standout story, a notable venture milestone, a big event or deadline). E.g.,
  "Meet 17 UBC ventures at the 2026 Venture Showcase, plus a new guide to commercializing
  your research" or "Last chance to apply for Venture Founder fall 2026, Web Summit
  highlights and the latest from UBC ventures".

Before presenting, check: if Option B could be swapped for Option A without the reader
learning anything new about the edition, rewrite it. Program applications outrank event
recaps for title real estate.

Avoid this (November 2026, too similar):
- Option A: "Meet the 17 UBC companies presenting at Innovation UBC's 2026 Venture Showcase"
- Option B: "Connect with 17 UBC companies at Innovation UBC's 2026 Venture Showcase"

## Working Document Scaffold

The Word doc opens with a short header block:

```
Target publish date: [Day, Month D, YYYY]

View last newsletter for reference: [URL]

**Suggested newsletter title**
- Option A: [...]
- Option B: [...]
```

Then the sections follow in order, each under a heading: Top Story, Upcoming Events &
Programs, Stories, Recent Venture & Researcher News, Community Events & Opportunities (with
Events and Opportunities sub-headers), Resource Spotlight. Sections 1-3 keep their heading
and stay empty if no Innovation UBC-owned content qualifies. Highlighted Venture Newsletter
items may be mixed in; leave them alone.

## Review Checklist

Before presenting any draft, verify:

- [ ] All titles and headlines are in sentence case (official names and branded events excepted)
- [ ] No em dashes or double hyphens anywhere
- [ ] Canadian spelling
- [ ] Dates use three-letter months ("Nov 5", "Nov 5-6")
- [ ] Sections 1-3 contain only Innovation UBC-owned content (empty if none qualifies)
- [ ] No participant-only content in the General Newsletter (flag it for the Venture Newsletter)
- [ ] Upcoming Events & Programs, Stories, and Resource Spotlight descriptions are under 30 words (unless approved)
- [ ] Upcoming Events & Programs descriptions speak to the reader
- [ ] Stories descriptions are 2 sentences, and sentence 2 invites the reader into the piece (unless it's a pure announcement)
- [ ] Descriptions don't repeat the item's title back at the reader
- [ ] CTAs are specific and carry their link inline after a colon ("Read:", "Watch:", "Photos:", "RSVP:", "Learn more:")
- [ ] Venture & Researcher News bullets lead with the venture or person, aim for one line, have no closing period, skip "UBC spin-off", and have a verified UBC connection
- [ ] Community Events & Opportunities are in date order with "Ongoing" last; event descriptors only where the name isn't informative
- [ ] Title Options A and B are distinct: A is the Top Story alone, B adds a tease of other items
- [ ] No filler phrases or corporate-speak
- [ ] URLs are clean (no markdown formatting around them)
- [ ] Any word-count or length constraint given for the item or section is met
